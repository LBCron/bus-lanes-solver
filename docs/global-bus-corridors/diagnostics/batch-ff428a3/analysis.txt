The strict coarse-plan/fine-batch proposal does not establish scalable completed routing at 24 or 32 lanes. Both algorithms completed 2 of 4 synthetic cases. The proposal used fewer fine expansions on its two successes but increased copper length and skew. The most useful actual-board result is a separate public-pipeline eligibility prototype: routing an untimed 8 signal A64 bus through existing negotiation completed the exact same final geometry much sooner than prior saved whole-core runs. That observation warrants a narrow production eligibility change and fresh paired validation; it is not itself a comparable end-to-end benchmark speedup.

Synthetic batch method
  Serial paired baseline negotiation versus coarse corridor plan plus at most 4 deduplicated lane orders. Each fine search treats supplied copper, all future terminal points, and all earlier lanes as hard continuous geometry. Coarse guide penalties are advisory. Full acceptance requires exact endpoint coverage and native continuous copper DRC. Both arms have 5000 cooperative-yield and 120 s external caps; batch additionally has 64000 fine expansions/route. These are unequal internal algorithm budgets. Fine expansion counts exclude coarse planner internals, whose yields are separately recorded. All timing values below are one descriptive fresh run/arm on Bun 1.4.3, not statistically repeated throughput measurements.

Geometry qualification
  Original pocket24 and pocket32 fixtures were physically infeasible: necessary x=0 single-layer cut capacity was 23 (3 center + 10 in each bypass). Their failures cannot demonstrate solver scaling limits. The tested capacity-expanded padding max(2.2,.15*n+.4) raises necessary cut upper bounds to 29/41/53 for 16/24/32. This removes that particular obstruction; it does not prove full geometry feasible. Full derivation: batch-corridor-space-analysis.txt.
